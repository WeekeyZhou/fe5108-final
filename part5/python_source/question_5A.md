# 5A Factor composite definitions

MSCI combines book value/price, forward 12-month earnings/price and dividend yield in its value score. Growth uses long- and short-term forecast EPS growth, internal growth (ROE multiplied by the earnings-retention ratio), and historical EPS and sales-per-share trends. Standard-index growth scores double-weight long-term forecast growth. Value and growth are separate dimensions; stocks can exhibit both or neither and may receive partial inclusion in both indexes.

The rules vary: Small Cap omits long-term forecast growth; Banks and Diversified Financials omit sales growth, except Multi-Sector Holdings and Financial Exchanges & Data. Missing signals are excluded and remaining weights renormalized. Share classes can differ in internal growth because dividend payouts differ. These rules reflect differences in accounting relevance and data availability; that rationale is an economic interpretation of the specified exceptions.

Fama-French HML uses book-to-market alone: half the sum of small-value and big-value portfolio returns minus half the sum of small-growth and big-growth returns. HML is a long-short spread across size groups; MSCI USA Value is a long-only, free-float-weighted style index within the US large/mid-cap universe. Its growth classification is richer than simply low book-to-market.
